# Dynasty Fund Research Summary

## Executive Summary

A dynasty fund is a perpetual endowment designed to provide income to a growing family lineage indefinitely. This research explores the key financial, demographic, and actuarial factors that determine whether such a fund can be sustainable.

## Key Research Findings

### 1. Perpetual Withdrawal Rates

The foundational research comes from the **Trinity Study** (Cooley, Hubbard, and Walz, 1998), which examined sustainable withdrawal rates from retirement portfolios. Key findings:

- The famous **4% rule** was designed for a **30-year** horizon, not perpetuity
- For truly perpetual income, research suggests **3-3.5%** withdrawal rates
- University endowments typically use 4-5% with smoothing mechanisms (e.g., University of Michigan averages over 28 quarters)
- Administrative costs for dynasty trusts typically consume **1-3%** of assets annually

**Source**: [Trinity Study - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_study)

### 2. Investment Returns

Historical S&P 500 performance (inflation-adjusted):

| Period | Real Annual Return |
|--------|-------------------|
| 30-year average | 7.7% |
| 50-year average | 8.0% |
| 100+ year average | 7.0-7.2% |

Key insight: After accounting for inflation, long-term equity returns average approximately **7%** annually.

**Source**: [Trade That Swing - Historical Returns](https://tradethatswing.com/average-historical-stock-market-returns-for-sp-500-5-year-up-to-150-year-averages/)

### 3. U.S. Median Household Income

Current data (2024):
- **Median household income**: $83,730
- **Historical real growth**: ~0.5-1% annually above inflation
- From 1984 to 2023: grew from $58,930 to $80,610 (37% real increase over 39 years ≈ 0.8%/year)

**Source**: [FRED - Real Median Household Income](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/MEHOINUSA672N)

### 4. Demographic Variables

#### Marriage Age (2022-2023)
- Men: **30.2 years** median age at first marriage
- Women: **28.4 years** median age at first marriage
- Historical trend: Rising steadily since 1950s (was 22.5/20.1 for men/women)

**Source**: [Census Bureau Historical Marital Status Tables](https://www.census.gov/data/tables/time-series/demo/families/marital.html)

#### Fertility Rate
- Current U.S. Total Fertility Rate: **1.6 children per woman**
- Replacement rate: 2.1
- Projected to remain at ~1.6 through 2055

**Source**: [CBO Demographic Outlook](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/61164)

#### Life Expectancy (2022)
- At birth: **77.5 years** overall (74.8 male, 80.2 female)
- At age 65: ~19 additional years
- Probability of surviving to age 80: ~55%

**Source**: [SSA Actuarial Life Table](https://www.ssa.gov/oact/STATS/table4c6.html)

### 5. Multigenerational Wealth Challenges

Research shows significant challenges for perpetual wealth:
- **60%** of wealthy families exhaust most of their estate by the second generation
- **90%** of family fortunes are gone by the third generation
- Less than 3% of failures stem from poor investment returns - most are behavioral

**Source**: [PMC - Generations of Advantage](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6296851/)

## Critical Success Factors

For a dynasty fund to succeed:

1. **Conservative withdrawal rates** (~3% for true perpetuity)
2. **Growth must exceed**: withdrawals + inflation + income growth + family expansion
3. **Governance structure** to manage behavioral risks
4. **Administrative efficiency** to minimize costs

## Mathematical Implications

The core sustainability equation:
```
Fund Growth Rate > Withdrawal Rate + Inflation + Income Growth Rate + Family Growth Rate
```

With typical values:
- Investment return: 7% real
- Sustainable withdrawal: 3-3.5%
- Income growth: 0.5-1% real
- Family growth: Depends on fertility rate and family structure

The key tension is between:
- Fund growth (limited by market returns)
- Beneficiary growth (exponential with each generation)

This suggests that without constraints on beneficiary growth, almost any dynasty fund will eventually become unsustainable.
